# Source-Grounded Study & Practice

Study-material-grounded exercise generation and practice application

**Mohammadhossein Zare**

Project ID: 2026-SD-03

---

<!-- 2xl -->
## Problem

- Students study from personal notes and documents, but questions generated by AI are not always supported by those sources.
- Practice results, revisions and learning history often become scattered across files or conversations.
- A useful exercise must remain **verifiable, editable and reusable** as the source material evolves.

**Core question:** How can study material become trustworthy, reusable practice?

---

<!-- 2xl -->
## Target users

**Primary user: an individual learner**

- Uploads textual material for one subject or course.
- Reviews and corrects AI-suggested questions before practice.
- Answers exercises, checks feedback and returns to previous attempts.

**Initial boundary:** no teacher/admin workflow, multi-user collaboration or formal examination.

---

<!-- 2xl -->
## Planned scope — first implementation

**Input:** Textual study material and defined study units/topics.

**Practice:** Multiple-choice and short-answer questions, including reference answers and source excerpts.

**Control:** Editable drafts, approval before questions enter the bank, reporting of faulty questions and assessments.

**Progress:** Saved attempts, topic-level results and source-change review status.

---

<!-- 2xl -->
## Planned scope — persistent study environment

A **Study Workspace** keeps sources, sessions, questions, attempts and plans together.

- Resume study without rebuilding the context of every conversation.
- Reference saved questions and sources directly from chat.
- Keep question and attempt records independently of the AI model.
- Support the same study state on desktop and mobile.

The broader vision also includes a **Global Study Coordinator**, memory and mind maps; these are staged behind the core exercise workflow.

---

<!-- 2xl -->
## Initial user journey — create practice

1. Select a subject and add study material.
2. Choose a topic or study unit.
3. Generate MCQ or short-answer **draft questions with source references**.
4. Review, edit or reject suggested questions.
5. Approve valid questions into the exercise bank.
6. Assemble an exercise set with the requested coverage and repetition constraints.

---

<!-- 2xl -->
## Initial user journey — practice and maintain

1. Answer a question set; pause and resume if needed.
2. Score MCQs from stored answer keys.
3. Assess short answers against explicit criteria and flag uncertainty.
4. Save feedback, results and previous attempts.
5. When a source changes, identify dependent questions that need review.

**Evidence matters:** question quality and traceability are evaluated separately from claims about learning improvement.
